# Regression Modelling and Optimisation of Plasma Parameters for Controlled Photon Emission in PE-ALD

## Overview

This repository contains the Python analysis and modelling tools developed for my BSc Physics final-year project at the University of York. The written report is included as `report_final.pdf`.

The project investigates how plasma operating conditions, specifically input power and gas pressure, influence photon emission in an oxygen plasma used for plasma-enhanced atomic layer deposition (PE-ALD). The primary objective was to test whether simple regression models can accurately describe photon emission behaviour and be used to find operating conditions that control the balance between vacuum ultraviolet (VUV) and non-VUV emission.

This matters for PE-ALD because VUV photons can influence surface reactions and can damage sensitive substrates, even when ion energies are low.

Photon emission data were generated by a zero-dimensional plasma-chemical kinetics model of Ar/O₂ inductively coupled plasmas (Engelbrecht et al., 2024). The simulation software is proprietary and is not included. This repository contains the scripts used to process, model and analyse its output, together with the processed data.

## Project Objectives

- Investigate how oxygen plasma photon emission varies with input power and pressure.
- Compare several regression models for their suitability in describing emission trends.
- Evaluate model performance on training and validation datasets.
- Develop regression-based methods for predicting emission under new operating conditions.
- Analyse the relationship between VUV and non-VUV photon flux.
- Identify operating conditions that maximise or minimise the VUV-to-non-VUV flux ratio.

## Simulation Data

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Plasma model | Zero-dimensional, volume-averaged Ar/O₂ ICP model (Engelbrecht et al., 2024) |
| Reactor geometry | 65 mm diameter, 100 mm plasma length (Kessels et al., 2007), surface-to-volume ratio ≈ 120 m⁻¹ |
| Power range | 100–2000 W |
| Pressure range | 2–750 mTorr (0.3–100 Pa) |
| 1D power sweep | Pressure fixed at 10 mTorr |
| 1D pressure sweep | Power fixed at 500 W |
| 2D grid | 5 powers (200–2000 W) × 10 pressures (5–200 mTorr), 50 points |
| Output | Photon emission rates (m⁻³ s⁻¹) for 12 oxygen lines |

The regression models were trained on the lower part of each 1D range (power 100–1000 W, pressure 2–400 mTorr) and validated against the extended datasets (power up to 2000 W, pressure up to 750 mTorr). This tests how well each model carries over to a wider parameter range.

## Methodology

### 1. Data Analysis

Simulation output is processed to extract the stabilised (final time-step) emission rate for each of the 12 lines at every power or pressure setting. These are saved to CSV and plotted, individually and grouped by transition and by VUV / non-VUV.

### 2. Regression Modelling

One-dimensional models (x is power or pressure):

| Model | Form |
|---|---|
| Linear | $I = mx + c$ |
| Power law | $I = Ax^{\alpha}$ |
| Saturation | $I = \dfrac{Ax^{\alpha}}{1+Bx}$ |
| **Extended saturation** | $I = \dfrac{Ax^{\alpha}}{1+Bx+Cx^{2}}$ |
| Exponential suppression | $I = Ax^{\alpha}e^{-\gamma x}$ |

Models were fitted with `scipy.optimize.curve_fit` and compared using the coefficient of determination ($R^2$) on training and validation data. The linear and power-law models cannot reproduce the pressure peak and were not used for the pressure data.

Minimum $R^2$ across all 12 lines:

| Power scan | Training | Validation |
|---|---:|---:|
| Linear | 0.981613 | N/A |
| Power law | 0.971568 | N/A |
| Saturation | 0.998422 | 0.950009 |
| **Extended saturation** | **0.999995** | **0.984202** |
| Exponential suppression | 0.999856 | 0.970786 |

| Pressure scan | Training | Validation |
|---|---:|---:|
| Saturation | 0.903586 | 0.927114 |
| **Extended saturation** | **0.995535** | **0.996308** |
| Exponential suppression | 0.944661 | 0.952461 |

Two-dimensional models considered in the report:

| Model | Script | Min. $R^2$ |
|---|---|---:|
| Combined 2D extended: $\dfrac{Ap^{\alpha}P^{\beta}}{1+Bp+CP+Dp^{2}+EP^{2}}$ | `fit_2d_grid.py` | 0.983519 |
| 2D exponential: $Ap^{\alpha}P^{\beta}e^{-Bp-CP}$ | `fit_2d_grid_exponential.py` | 0.961424 |
| **2D extended with interaction term (log space):** $\dfrac{Ap^{\alpha}P^{\beta}}{1+Bp+CP+Dp^{2}+EP^{2}+FpP}$ | `fit_2d_grid_interaction.py` | **0.995926** |

A single interpolation test at 1800 W, 180 mTorr (not in the training grid) gave worst-case line errors of 25.3 %, 13.6 % and 9.8 % for the three models respectively. A log-space version of the interaction model reduced the worst-case error to 9.3 % (best case 1.13 %). `fit_2d_grid_interaction.py` fits $\ln I = \ln A + \alpha\ln p + \beta\ln P - \ln(1+Bp+CP+Dp^{2}+EP^{2}+FpP)$, so it minimises relative rather than absolute error and reports `R^2_log`. This is not directly comparable with the linear-space $R^2$ of the other two scripts.

### 3. Photon Flux Optimisation

The fitted models are used to study total photon flux and the VUV-to-non-VUV flux ratio:

$$R=\frac{\Phi_{\mathrm{VUV}}}{\Phi_{\mathrm{non\text{-}VUV}}}$$

For the extended model, the pressure (or power) of maximum emission follows from $dI/dx = 0$, which reduces to a quadratic:

$$C(\alpha-2)\,x^{2} + B(\alpha-1)\,x + \alpha = 0$$

Totals and ratios across a range are found by summing the fitted line intensities and scanning a 2000-point grid.

## Key Results

- The extended saturation model was the most suitable one-dimensional model for both power and pressure, with minimum training $R^2$ above 0.99.
- The two-dimensional model with an interaction term reproduced the combined power-pressure space with a minimum $R^2$ of approximately 0.996 and a worst-case interpolation error below 10 %.
- Pressure is the more influential parameter for the VUV / non-VUV balance, whereas power mainly scales absolute intensity.
- Adjusting pressure and power together gives far greater control over both total flux and spectral composition than either alone.

One-dimensional results from the fitted extended models:

| Scan | Total flux peak | VUV / non-VUV ratio |
|---|---|---|
| Power, 100–2000 W (10 mTorr) | Rises throughout the range, maximum at 2000 W | Falls from 0.77 (100 W) to 0.42 (2000 W) |
| Pressure, 2–200 mTorr (500 W) | 10.6 mTorr (VUV peaks at 9.3, non-VUV at 11.0) | Minimum 0.53 at 13.4 mTorr, maximum 1.14 at 200 mTorr |

Across the two-dimensional parameter space, the VUV / non-VUV ratio varies between:

| Operating conditions | VUV / non-VUV ratio |
|---|---:|
| 200 mTorr, 100 W | 1.51 |
| 2 mTorr, 2000 W | 0.36 |

## Repository Contents

Scripts are listed in the order they are run. The twin power / pressure scripts do the same job on different datasets.

### 1D pipeline

| Step | File | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `analysis.py` | Reads simulation output (`data_to_analyse/<run>/K.csv`), extracts stabilised emission rates for the selected reactions, saves plots to `initial_plots/` and writes `analysed_data.csv`. |
| 2 | `regression_power.py`, `regression_pressure.py` | Fit the linear, power-law, saturation, extended and exponential models to the training data. Save fit plots and `power_regression_results.csv` / `pressure_regression_results.csv`. |
| 3 | `regression_validation_power.py`, `regression_validation_pressure.py` | Evaluate the fitted parameters against the extended dataset and report validation $R^2$ for each model and line. |
| 4 | `power_vuv_nonvuv_analysis.py`, `pressure_vuv_nonvuv_analysis.py` | Sum VUV and non-VUV flux from the extended model, evaluate the ratio across the range and locate the peak total flux and the ratio extrema. The pressure version also finds the peak VUV and non-VUV flux. |
| 5 | `find_extended_peak.py` | Solves for the pressure (or power) and intensity at which each line peaks, using the extended-model parameters. |
| 6 | `predict_flux.py` | Predicts line fluxes at a chosen pressure and power by combining the 1D pressure and power models under a separability assumption. |

### 2D pipeline (`mesh_prediction/`)

The scripts in `mesh_prediction/` work directly on the 2D simulation grid (5 powers × 10 pressures). Each model has a fitting script and a matching prediction script. The report's final model is the log-space interaction model.

| Model | Fit script | Prediction script | Fit output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Combined 2D rational (no interaction term) | `fit_2d_grid.py` | `predict_from_fit.py` | `fits_and_plots/fitting_results.csv` |
| 2D exponential (separable) | `fit_2d_grid_exponential.py` | `predict_from_fit_exponential.py` | `fits_and_plots_exponential/fitting_exponential.csv` |
| **2D rational with interaction term, fitted in log space** | `fit_2d_grid_interaction.py` | `predict_from_fit_interaction.py` | `fits_log/fitting_interaction_log.csv` |

| File | Description |
|---|---|
| `fit_2d_grid*.py` | Read the final row of every `K.csv` on the grid, fit each of the 12 emission lines, write the parameters and $R^2$ to CSV, and save a contour plot per line plus VUV, non-VUV and total surfaces. |
| `predict_from_fit*.py` | Read a fit CSV, prompt for a pressure and power, and print the predicted flux for each line with VUV, non-VUV and grand totals. |
| `find_peaks_2d.py` | Takes the interaction-model CSV and searches a 300 × 300 grid for the peak VUV, non-VUV and total flux and the maximum VUV / non-VUV and non-VUV / VUV ratios. Saves a contour plot for each, with the peak marked. |

An alternative that does not need the 2D grid is also included:

| File | Description |
|---|---|
| `predict_flux_2D.py` | Builds a single 2D rational model per line from the two 1D fits, constrained to reproduce both at their reference slices (no interaction term), and predicts fluxes at a chosen pressure and power. |

## Installation

The scripts were developed with Python 3. Install the dependencies with:

```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt
```

## Usage

### 1. Data analysis

```bash
python analysis.py
```

`analysis.py` is configured for the power scan. For the pressure scan, change `independent_var_name` (to `"Pressure (mTorr)"`) and `independent_var_values` at the top of the script. Run folder names must start with the numeric value followed by the unit letter (`w` for power, `m` for pressure), as this is how runs are sorted. The output `analysed_data.csv` should be renamed to match the filenames in the table above.

### 2. Regression

```bash
python regression_power.py
python regression_pressure.py
```

Each script fits all models to every emission line and writes the fit parameters and $R^2$ values to a results CSV, plus one plot per line.

### 3. Validation

Copy the results from step 2 to `power_regression_results_initial.csv` and `pressure_regression_results_initial.csv`, then run:

```bash
python regression_validation_power.py
python regression_validation_pressure.py
```

Outputs are `power_validation_results.csv` / `pressure_validation_results.csv` and a comparison plot per line.

### 4. VUV / non-VUV analysis

```bash
python power_vuv_nonvuv_analysis.py power_regression_results.csv
python pressure_vuv_nonvuv_analysis.py pressure_regression_results.csv
```

These evaluate the fitted extended models over 100–2000 W and 2–200 mTorr respectively. They print the peak total flux and the maximum and minimum VUV / non-VUV ratios and save a plot of VUV, non-VUV and total flux with the ratio on a secondary axis.

### 5. Finding emission peaks

```bash
python find_extended_peak.py pressure_regression_results.csv
```

The input CSV must contain `Emission`, `Extended A`, `Extended alpha`, `Extended B` and `Extended C`. The script prints the peak location and peak intensity for each line, or "No peak" where the fitted model has none.

### 6. Predicting photon flux from the 1D models

```bash
python predict_flux.py pressure_regression_results.csv power_regression_results.csv
```

The script prompts for the target pressure (mTorr) and power (W), then for the reference conditions of the two scans: the power held during the pressure scan (500 W) and the pressure held during the power scan (10 mTorr). It prints per-line fluxes and VUV, non-VUV and grand totals, combining the two fits as

$$I(p,P)=\frac{I_{\mathrm{press}}(p)\,I_{\mathrm{pow}}(P)}{\sqrt{I_{\mathrm{press}}(p_0)\,I_{\mathrm{pow}}(P_0)}}$$

It also prints a cross-check of $I_{\mathrm{press}}(p_0)$ against $I_{\mathrm{pow}}(P_0)$. These should agree if the separability assumption holds.

### 7. 2D grid fitting and prediction (`mesh_prediction/`)

**Fit.** Each fit script takes the root folder of the 2D grid data:

```bash
python mesh_prediction/fit_2d_grid.py <data_root>
python mesh_prediction/fit_2d_grid_exponential.py <data_root>
python mesh_prediction/fit_2d_grid_interaction.py <data_root>
```

`<data_root>` contains one folder per power (e.g. `200w`), each holding one folder per pressure named `<power>_<pressure>` (e.g. `200_5`) with a `K.csv` inside. The scripts read the last row of each `K.csv` directly, so `analysis.py` is not needed for the 2D data. Results are written to a new folder inside `<data_root>` (see Generated Outputs).

The rational and exponential scripts fit in linear space with several starting guesses and discard a line if its best $R^2$ is 0.5 or lower. A discarded line is missing from the CSV, so the totals will be incomplete. The prediction scripts print a warning if `r549` is absent.

**Predict.** Pass the matching fit CSV to the prediction script and enter a pressure (mTorr) and power (W) when prompted:

```bash
python mesh_prediction/predict_from_fit_interaction.py <data_root>/fits_log/fitting_interaction_log.csv
python mesh_prediction/predict_from_fit.py <data_root>/fits_and_plots/fitting_results.csv
python mesh_prediction/predict_from_fit_exponential.py <data_root>/fits_and_plots_exponential/fitting_exponential.csv
```

**Find peaks.** Use the interaction-model CSV:

```bash
python mesh_prediction/find_peaks_2d.py <data_root>/fits_log/fitting_interaction_log.csv
```

The search covers 2–200 mTorr and 100–2000 W (`P_MIN`, `P_MAX`, `POWER_MIN`, `POWER_MAX` at the top of the script). It prints the pressure, power and value of each peak, and saves five contour plots next to the CSV, named `<csv name>_vuv_total.png`, `_nonvuv_total.png`, `_total_flux.png`, `_vuv_ratio.png` and `_nonvuv_ratio.png`.

**Slice-constrained alternative.** `predict_flux_2D.py` needs only the two 1D results CSVs and prompts for the same target and reference conditions as `predict_flux.py`:

```bash
python predict_flux_2D.py pressure_regression_results.csv power_regression_results.csv
```

It uses the exponents α and β from the 1D fits and solves for the denominator coefficients so the 2D surface matches both 1D fits at the reference slices. It has no interaction term, so its predictions will not exactly match the grid-fitted models above.

## Limitations and Scope

- The plasma simulation software is proprietary and not included. This repository covers analysis, regression modelling and optimisation, not generation of the original data.
- All results are based on one simulation model and have not been compared with experimental measurements.
- Predictions outside the simulated ranges (100–2000 W, 2–750 mTorr) are extrapolations. Ratio and total-flux trends at the range limits (for example the 200 mTorr, 100 W maximum ratio) are likely to continue beyond them, so these extremes are boundaries of the studied region, not true optima.
- The combined pressure-power prediction in `predict_flux.py` assumes pressure and power dependencies are separable. This is an approximation, not a fully coupled physical model.
- The two-dimensional validation consists of a single interpolation test point (1800 W, 180 mTorr), and the 777 nm O(5p) lines were predicted least accurately.
- The 2D models are fitted to a grid spanning 200–2000 W and 5–200 mTorr, but `find_peaks_2d.py` searches 100–2000 W and 2–200 mTorr. The reported ratio extremes (1.51 at 200 mTorr, 100 W and 0.36 at 2 mTorr, 2000 W) sit at corners of that search window, partly outside the fitted grid, so they are extrapolated edge values.
- The regression models are empirical and not derived from the plasma kinetics. A more physically informed model and a denser simulation grid would be the natural next steps.
